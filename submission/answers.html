How the SQL part was done

No data was provided, so I built a mock PostgreSQL database that follows the schema and Appendix A exactly (10 tables, 100 S shops, 100 X shops, 60 dual pairs, 2,500 products, 37,479 orders, months 2026-07 to 2026-10), then audited it against Appendix A (18 checks, file sql/00b_audit_vs_appendix.sql) before writing the queries. The mock data contain deliberate edge cases (a shop that becomes dual mid-period, a missing crawl month, shop-months where crawler and market research disagree) so the queries are tested against them. All queries run as-is on that database; the result files in results/ are their output. Dialect: PostgreSQL. T-SQL differences are noted in the files.

Assumptions shared by Q1 to Q3

  • Last 3 months = the three latest months in the data: 2026-08, 2026-09, 2026-10. Hard-coded for readability; a dynamic version is noted in the files.
  • Sold = order_status = 'complete'. Created / paid / on_delivery are not final; canceled / returned are not sales. X crawler GMV also cannot contain canceled orders, so this keeps S and X comparable.
  • GMV as defined in Appendix A: before discount and shipping deduction (item_gmv, gmv). All values in USD.
  • Days for ADG / ADO = calendar days of the month (31 / 30 / 31), not days with sales. ABS = GMV / orders. Tier bounds: lower inclusive, upper exclusive.
  • Top-N uses ROW_NUMBER(), so exactly N rows per month; ties at the cut-off are broken arbitrarily.

A1. Top 100 selling items each month (by GMV), company S

Business question: which products carry S's GMV each month, at what price, and how many orders.

Specific assumptions. Item = s_product_id; details from dim_s_product (name, brand, category lv1/lv2). Average selling price = quantity-weighted average of discounted_price (what the buyer paid), not the listing price; min / max = min / max of discounted_price. order_count = COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) because a product can appear on more than one item row of the same order.

Columns in the file: order_month, rank_in_month, s_product_id, product_name, brand, category_lv1, category_lv2, avg_price, min_price, max_price, gmv, order_count. Full result: results/q1_result.csv.

Query (sql/02_q1_top100_items.sql):

WITH item_month AS (
    SELECT i.order_month, i.s_product_id, p.product_name, p.brand, p.category_lv1, p.category_lv2,
           SUM(i.item_gmv)                                                           AS gmv,
           COUNT(DISTINCT i.order_id)                                                AS order_count,
           ROUND(SUM(i.discounted_price * i.quantity)::numeric / SUM(i.quantity), 2) AS avg_price,
           MIN(i.discounted_price)                                                   AS min_price,
           MAX(i.discounted_price)                                                   AS max_price
    FROM fact_s_order_item AS i
    JOIN fact_s_order   AS o ON o.order_id     = i.order_id
    JOIN dim_s_product  AS p ON p.s_product_id = i.s_product_id
    WHERE o.order_status = 'complete'
      AND i.order_month IN ('2026-08', '2026-09', '2026-10')
    GROUP BY i.order_month, i.s_product_id, p.product_name, p.brand, p.category_lv1, p.category_lv2
),
ranked AS (
    SELECT *, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY order_month ORDER BY gmv DESC) AS rank_in_month
    FROM item_month
)
SELECT order_month, rank_in_month, s_product_id, product_name, brand, category_lv1, category_lv2,
       avg_price, min_price, max_price, gmv, order_count
FROM ranked
WHERE rank_in_month <= 100
ORDER BY order_month, rank_in_month;

Why two CTEs: a window function cannot sit in WHERE, so the rank is computed first and filtered in the next step. T-SQL: identical; replace ::numeric with CAST(... AS decimal).

A2. Top 20 dual shops in S each month, with ADG / ADO / ABS / ABS tier

Business question: among sellers already on both platforms, who are S's biggest 20 each month and what does their basket look like.

Specific assumptions. Dual = the S shop has a row in fact_s_x_monthly_shop_mapping for that month (the mapping is monthly, so dual status is evaluated per month: a shop can be dual in Sep and not in Aug). GMV and order count come from fact_s_order (one row per order). x_shop_id is carried from the mapping so Q3 can join X data directly.

Query (sql/03_q2_top20_dual_shops.sql):

WITH shop_month AS (                      -- S performance per (shop, month), dual shops only
    SELECT o.order_month, o.s_shop_id, m.x_shop_id,
           SUM(o.gmv) AS gmv, COUNT(*) AS order_count
    FROM fact_s_order AS o
    JOIN fact_s_x_monthly_shop_mapping AS m
      ON  m.s_shop_id     = o.s_shop_id
      AND m.mapping_month = o.order_month   -- dual status is per month
    WHERE o.order_status = 'complete'
      AND o.order_month IN ('2026-08', '2026-09', '2026-10')
    GROUP BY o.order_month, o.s_shop_id, m.x_shop_id
),
metrics AS (
    SELECT sm.*,
           EXTRACT(DAY FROM (TO_DATE(sm.order_month || '-01', 'YYYY-MM-DD')
                             + INTERVAL '1 month' - INTERVAL '1 day'))::int AS days_in_month,
           ROUND(sm.gmv::numeric / sm.order_count, 2)                      AS abs
    FROM shop_month AS sm
),
ranked AS (
    SELECT mt.*, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY mt.order_month ORDER BY mt.gmv DESC) AS rank_in_month
    FROM metrics AS mt
)
SELECT r.order_month, r.rank_in_month, r.s_shop_id, s.shop_name, s.seller_type, r.x_shop_id,
       r.days_in_month, r.gmv, r.order_count,
       ROUND(r.gmv::numeric         / r.days_in_month, 2) AS adg,
       ROUND(r.order_count::numeric / r.days_in_month, 2) AS ado,
       r.abs,
       CASE WHEN r.abs < 5  THEN 'under 5 USD'
            WHEN r.abs < 10 THEN '5 - 10 USD'
            WHEN r.abs < 50 THEN '10 - 50 USD'
            ELSE                 'over 50 USD' END AS abs_tier
FROM ranked AS r
JOIN dim_s_shop AS s ON s.s_shop_id = r.s_shop_id
WHERE r.rank_in_month <= 20
ORDER BY r.order_month, r.rank_in_month;

T-SQL: days_in_month = DAY(EOMONTH(CAST(order_month + '-01' AS date))). Checks run: 20 rows per month; days = 31/30/31; abs × orders ≈ gmv; a shop that is dual only from September does not appear in August.

A3. Extend A2 with X shop performance (crawled_fact_x_shop)

Business question: for S's top 20 dual sellers, how does the same seller perform on X in the same month, side by side.

Specific assumptions. X row matched on x_shop_id (from the monthly mapping) and crawled_month = order_month. LEFT JOIN: a top-20 S shop stays in the list even when the crawler has no row that month; X metrics are NULL and tier = 'no X data'. Dropping it would silently change the top 20 and hide a data gap. X metrics are crawler estimates: gaps under ~10-15% vs S should not be read as real differences. Same formulas and tier thresholds on both sides. gmv_ratio_x_vs_s added as a convenience (> 1 = seller sells more on X).

Query (sql/04_q3_s_vs_x_performance.sql):

WITH shop_month AS (                      -- same as A2
    SELECT o.order_month, o.s_shop_id, m.x_shop_id,
           SUM(o.gmv) AS s_gmv, COUNT(*) AS s_order_count
    FROM fact_s_order AS o
    JOIN fact_s_x_monthly_shop_mapping AS m
      ON m.s_shop_id = o.s_shop_id AND m.mapping_month = o.order_month
    WHERE o.order_status = 'complete'
      AND o.order_month IN ('2026-08', '2026-09', '2026-10')
    GROUP BY o.order_month, o.s_shop_id, m.x_shop_id
),
ranked AS (
    SELECT sm.*,
           EXTRACT(DAY FROM (TO_DATE(sm.order_month || '-01', 'YYYY-MM-DD')
                             + INTERVAL '1 month' - INTERVAL '1 day'))::int AS days_in_month,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY sm.order_month ORDER BY sm.s_gmv DESC) AS rank_in_month
    FROM shop_month AS sm
),
top20 AS (SELECT * FROM ranked WHERE rank_in_month <= 20),
joined AS (                               -- new in A3: X performance for the same shop-month
    SELECT t.*, x.estimated_gmv AS x_gmv, x.estimated_order_count AS x_order_count
    FROM top20 AS t
    LEFT JOIN crawled_fact_x_shop AS x
      ON x.x_shop_id = t.x_shop_id AND x.crawled_month = t.order_month
)
SELECT j.order_month, j.rank_in_month, j.s_shop_id, s.shop_name, s.seller_type,
       j.x_shop_id, xd.shop_name AS x_shop_name, j.days_in_month,
       j.s_gmv, j.s_order_count,
       ROUND(j.s_gmv::numeric         / j.days_in_month, 2)  AS s_adg,
       ROUND(j.s_order_count::numeric / j.days_in_month, 2)  AS s_ado,
       ROUND(j.s_gmv::numeric         / j.s_order_count, 2)  AS s_abs,
       CASE WHEN j.s_gmv::numeric / j.s_order_count < 5  THEN 'under 5 USD'
            WHEN j.s_gmv::numeric / j.s_order_count < 10 THEN '5 - 10 USD'
            WHEN j.s_gmv::numeric / j.s_order_count < 50 THEN '10 - 50 USD'
            ELSE 'over 50 USD' END                           AS s_abs_tier,
       j.x_gmv, j.x_order_count,
       ROUND(j.x_gmv::numeric         / j.days_in_month, 2)  AS x_adg,
       ROUND(j.x_order_count::numeric / j.days_in_month, 2)  AS x_ado,
       ROUND(j.x_gmv::numeric / NULLIF(j.x_order_count, 0), 2) AS x_abs,
       CASE WHEN j.x_gmv IS NULL OR j.x_order_count IS NULL OR j.x_order_count = 0 THEN 'no X data'
            WHEN j.x_gmv::numeric / j.x_order_count < 5  THEN 'under 5 USD'
            WHEN j.x_gmv::numeric / j.x_order_count < 10 THEN '5 - 10 USD'
            WHEN j.x_gmv::numeric / j.x_order_count < 50 THEN '10 - 50 USD'
            ELSE 'over 50 USD' END                           AS x_abs_tier,
       ROUND(j.x_gmv::numeric / NULLIF(j.s_gmv, 0), 2)       AS gmv_ratio_x_vs_s
FROM joined AS j
JOIN dim_s_shop AS s        ON s.s_shop_id  = j.s_shop_id
LEFT JOIN crawled_dim_x_shop AS xd ON xd.x_shop_id = j.x_shop_id
ORDER BY j.order_month, j.rank_in_month;

Checks run: still 60 rows after the LEFT JOIN; one x_shop_id per row (mapping is 1:1 per month); the shop whose crawl is missing in October shows NULL and 'no X data'; s_abs_tier equals A2's abs_tier for the same shop-month.

A4. Detecting and reconciling crawler vs market research disagreement on X GMV

Two estimates of the same thing (X shop GMV per month): the crawler infers GMV from public "sold" counters and prices (granular, timing-sensitive, starred as primary); the vendor model is aggregated and can lag. Neither is ground truth. The goal is not "the right number" but a rule, per shop-month, for which number to use and how much to trust it.

Detection criteria, in this order

#CriterionRuleRationale
C1CoverageShop-month exists in only one source → single_source. Not a disagreement; cannot be reconciled.You cannot compare what you do not have. Separate first or it pollutes the gap statistics (50 of 399 crawler rows in the mock data have no MR row).
C2Relative gapgap = |MR − crawler| / max(MR, crawler). <10% consistent; 10-25% watch; ≥25% conflict.Both sources carry ~10% error each; two errors combine to ~20%, so below 25% is noise. max() in the denominator keeps the gap symmetric whichever side is bigger.
C3Materiality floorApply C2 only when max(MR, crawler) ≥ USD 500 / month; else immaterial.A 60% gap on a USD 100 shop is a USD 60 problem. Keeps the review list short and actionable. Threshold is a parameter.
C4Persistenceconflict in ≥2 consecutive months → systematic; otherwise one_off.One bad month is a crawl hiccup; repeated conflict means a source is structurally wrong for that shop (wrong mapping, vendor model). Different fix, so different flag.
C5Global bias / driftMonthly mean and spread of (MR − crawler)/crawler across consistent shops; mean beyond ±5% or spread jumping → source_drift.If every shop moves the same way, a source changed, not the shops. Detected at population level.
C6Internal plausibilityPer source: implied ABS outside the shop's category price band; orders = 0 with GMV > 0; GMV jump > 3× vs the same source's prior month → implausible_<source>.A gap says that they differ, not which is wrong. Internal checks give a verdict on each source alone.
C7S-side triangulation (dual shops)Compare both X numbers with the same seller's S profile (GMV, ABS, orders). The source closer to S is more credible.Same seller, same products, same prices; S data are real transactions. Available for the ~60% of shops the business team cares about.

Reconciliation: which number to use

Never average blindly: averaging a good and a broken estimate yields a wrong number that looks reasonable. Pick one source per shop-month by rule and record the choice.

FlagsUseConfidenceWhy
consistent / watchCrawler (keep MR as reference)High / MediumAgree within noise; crawler is primary and more granular
conflict + implausible_crawlerMRMediumCrawler row is internally broken that month
conflict + implausible_mrCrawlerMediumVendor model is off for that shop
conflict, MR ≈ crawler of previous monthCrawlerMediumVendor lag (Appendix A: "possible lag vs real performance")
conflict, both plausible, dual shopSource closer to S profile (C7)MediumTriangulate with real data
conflict, both plausible, non-dual, one_offCrawler, flaggedLowDefault to primary; exclude from top-N views that month

Output is one table at shop-month grain: x_shop_id, month, crawler_gmv, mr_gmv, gap_pct, flags, gmv_final, source_used, confidence. Downstream queries (A3, dashboards) read gmv_final and confidence, never the raw sources, so every number traces to a rule. Operating rules: run the day after both sources land; review only conflict and source_drift; calibrate thresholds on 3-6 months of history (if >5% of material shop-months are in conflict, the bar is too tight); every systematic case must produce an upstream fix or it returns next month. On the mock data: 339 consistent, 7 watch, 3 conflict (all 2026-09, MR = 2.2× crawler, none persisting), 50 crawler-only, mean bias +1.7%, no drift. B. Projection model for company X: GMV, orders, P&L

Setup. X is assumed to be TikTok Shop Vietnam (second-largest platform, 41% of 4-platform GMV in 2025 per Metric). Quarterly model, Q1 2025A to Q4 2027E, three scenarios, USD at 26,000 VND/USD, P&L lines (1) to (13) exactly as Appendix B. Market GMV for a projected quarter = same quarter of the prior year × (1 + growth), so 2025 seasonality (Q4 ≈ 29% of the year) carries forward. X GMV = market × X share. Orders = GMV / AOV. Every input is blue, with its source or rationale beside it. The sheet has 9 tabs: README, Assumptions, Model, Scenarios, Findings, Sources, plus an extra S model and an S vs X comparison (not asked; added because "second largest" only means something relative to the largest).

Findings

  • GMV: USD 6.8bn (FY2025A) to USD 9.6-14.6bn in FY2027E; Base USD 12.1bn, 33% two-year CAGR. The worst-to-best spread (USD 5bn) is ~45% market size and ~55% share, so the share assumption matters more than the market forecast.
  • Seasonality: Q4 is ~29-32% of the year. Q4 2027E Base = USD 3.5bn in one quarter, more than Q1+Q2 2025 combined. Annual targets that ignore this under-plan Q4 fulfilment and subsidy by about a third.
  • Orders grow slower than GMV (1.7× vs 1.8×) because AOV rises with Mall mix. Peak ADO 6.5m orders/day in Q4 2027E vs 4.3m in Q4 2025: fulfilment capacity binds before demand.
  • Take rate is policy-driven, not volume-driven: 8.6% (Q1 2025) to 18.8% (Q4 2026E), +10pp, from three dated fee changes. After that revenue growth is pure volume.
  • P&L under Appendix B is contribution before fulfilment, payment, headcount and tech. X turns positive from Q2 2025 (after the April 2025 commission rise), FY2025A ~USD 130m (1.9% GMV), FY2027E Base USD 1.5bn (12.5%). A full P&L would be several points lower (Sea Ltd adj. EBITDA 0.7% of GMV).
  • Most sensitive inputs: subsidy (1pp of GMV = USD 121m in FY2027E; holding 2025 intensity cuts P&L to USD 1.2bn) and commission (40% of FY2027E revenue; a 2pp cut removes ~USD 240m, the size of the whole ads line).
  • Implication for S's seller acquisition: X's take rate now equals or exceeds S's, so "lower fees on X" no longer holds. S should sell fee stability and lower subsidy dependence to the Mall and high-ABS sellers surfaced in A2/A3, whose margins are hit hardest by a 6% transaction fee, and push before Q4 when X's subsidy budget is most stretched.

Caveats and sources

Market figures are third-party estimates (Metric, Q&Me) that differ by ~3 share points for the same period; Metric is used for quarterly alignment. AOV is derived, not disclosed. Fees are blended across categories from published ranges. Subsidy ratios are assumptions anchored on Shopee's marketing-to-GMV benchmark. Treat FY2027 outputs as ranges.
